## Summary

This is the complete text of a 619-page, fully open-access (CC BY 4.0) edited volume published by Springer as the capstone deliverable of the EU-funded COST Action FA1305 ("The EU Aquaponics Hub"), a 4-year (2014–2018) research network of 90 experts from 28 EU countries plus near-neighbour and international partners. Edited by Simon Goddek, Alyssa Joyce, Benz Kotzen and Gavin M. Burnell, the book assembles 24 independently authored chapters (68 contributing researchers/practitioners from 29 countries) into five parts: (I) framework conditions — global food challenges, resource limits, RAS and hydroponic technology basics; (II) aquaponics-specific technology — system basics, bacterial ecology, coupled vs. decoupled system design, nutrient cycling, sludge mineralisation, systems modelling, and alternative approaches (biofloc, aeroponics, algaeponics, vertical systems); (III) sustainability perspectives — fish diets, plant pathogens, aquaponics-integrated microgrids, and an "Anthropocene"/sustainability-first framing; (IV) management and marketing — fish health risk, commercial viability, organic-certification status, EU regulatory frameworks, and built-environment integration; and (V) education and social enterprise — aquaponics as a STEM teaching tool and as a vehicle for social enterprise and food-security interventions worldwide. No chapter in this volume reports a single controlled experiment conducted by "the book" as a unit — each chapter narratively synthesizes its authors' own prior work alongside the wider literature — so the book as a whole is extracted here as a narrative-review/technical-synthesis item with no trials.csv or plant.csv rows, consistent with CLAUDE.md's treatment of edited volumes.

> - **Region / system covered:** Global in subject matter, but institutionally and authorially centred on the EU COST Action FA1305 network (28 EU + 2 near-neighbour + 2 international-partner countries); individual chapters cite/discuss case studies from Europe, North America, Africa (South Africa, Namibia), the Middle East (Occupied Palestinian Territories, Gaza), and Australia.
> - **Argument:** Aquaponics — integrating recirculating aquaculture (RAS) and hydroponics — is an emerging, still-maturing food-production technology that can help address planetary resource boundaries (land, freshwater, nutrients), but faces unresolved scientific (nutrient cycling, pathogen control, energy use), economic (profitability, value chains), and regulatory (organic certification, EU legislation) challenges that the book's 24 chapters set out to characterise across the full technology-to-policy spectrum.

## Book structure and chapters

Part I — Framework Conditions in a Resource Limited World
1. **Aquaponics and Global Food Challenges** — S. Goddek, A. Joyce, B. Kotzen, M. Dos-Santos (p. 3). Frames aquaponics against planetary-boundary/food-security pressures; introduces decoupled aquaponics systems (DAPS) and sludge bioreactors as nutrient-recovery solutions; discusses system productivity, value chains, supply-chain management, and regulatory/food-safety hurdles. Read in full for this note.
2. **Aquaponics: Closing the Cycle on Limited Water, Land and Nutrient Resources** — A. Joyce, S. Goddek, B. Kotzen, S. Wuertz (p. 19). Positions aquaponics' origins in arid-region hydroponics and RAS development, and its role in reducing eutrophication/soil degradation via nutrient cycling.
3. **Recirculating Aquaculture Technologies** — C.A. Espinal, D. Matulić (p. 35). History, water-quality control processes, and technical developments of RAS as the aquaculture backbone of aquaponics.
4. **Hydroponic Technologies** — C. Maucieri, C. Nicoletto, E. van Os, D. Anseeuw, R. Van Havermaet, R. Junge (p. 77). Overview of hydroponic substrate/media types, nutrient solutions, and disinfection methods used in aquaponics designs.

Part II — Specific Aquaponics Technology
5. **Aquaponics: The Basics** — W. Lennard, S. Goddek (p. 113). Defines aquaponics via nutrient-resource-sharing principles; traces its emergence in the 1970s USA and European resurgence.
6. **Bacterial Relationships in Aquaponics: New Research Directions** — A. Joyce, M. Timmons, S. Goddek, T. Pentz (p. 145). Reviews microbiota roles across the RAS, hydroponic, and biofilter/sludge-digester (SDS) units.
7. **Coupled Aquaponics Systems** — H.W. Palm, U. Knaus, S. Appelbaum, S.M. Strauch, B. Kotzen (p. 163). Describes the "archetype" single-loop aquaponics design and its water-treatment/upscaling requirements; source of the book's working definition of aquaponics.
8. **Decoupled Aquaponics Systems** — S. Goddek, A. Joyce, S. Wuertz, O. Körner, I. Bläser, M. Reuter, K.J. Keesman (p. 201). Explains multi-loop DAPS rationale (separating fish/plant conditions, sludge reuse, phosphorus recovery) — closely related to sibling vault entries `goddekNecessityDesalinationTechnology2018` and `goddekImprovingNutrientWater2020`.
9. **Nutrient Cycling in Aquaponics Systems** — M. Eck, O. Körner, M.H. Jijakli (p. 231). Nitrogen/phosphorus cycling, mineralisation of solid fish faeces, and factors (pH, temperature, light) affecting nutrient availability.
10. **Aerobic and Anaerobic Treatments for Aquaponic Sludge Reduction and Mineralisation** — B. Delaide, H. Monsees, A. Gross, S. Goddek (p. 247). Microbial sludge-treatment routes for nutrient (especially phosphorus) recovery and bioenergy production.
11. **Aquaponics Systems Modelling** — K.J. Keesman, O. Körner, K. Wagner, J. Urban, D. Karimanzira, T. Rauschenbach, S. Goddek (p. 267). Systems-theory approach to dynamic modelling of fish tank, digester, and hydroponic subsystems for management/control.
12. **Aquaponics: Alternative Types and Approaches** — B. Kotzen, M.G.C. Emerenciano, N. Moheimani, G.M. Burnell (p. 301). Surveys allied technologies: algaeponics, aeroponics, aquaeroponics, maraponics, haloponics, biofloc technology, vertical aquaponics.

Part III — Perspective for Sustainable Development
13. **Fish Diets in Aquaponics** — L. Robaina, J. Pirhonen, E. Mente, J. Sánchez, N. Goosen (p. 333). Discusses tailoring fish-feed nitrogen/phosphorus/mineral content to jointly meet fish and plant nutrient needs.
14. **Plant Pathogens and Control Strategies in Aquaponics** — G. Stouvenakers, P. Dapprich, S. Massart, M.H. Jijakli (p. 353). Soil-borne pathogens (*Fusarium*, *Phytophthora*, *Pythium*) and the limits of curative treatment in coupled systems given fish/biofilter toxicity constraints.
15. **Smarthoods: Aquaponics Integrated Microgrids** — F. de Graaf, S. Goddek (p. 379). Proposes integrating decoupled multi-loop aquaponics with neighbourhood renewable-energy microgrids.
16. **Aquaponics for the Anthropocene: Towards a 'Sustainability First' Agenda** — J. Gott, R. Morgenstern, M. Turnšek (p. 393). Critical/positioning chapter on aquaponics research culture amid "hype" versus demonstrated outcomes.

Part IV — Management and Marketing
17. **Insight into Risks in Aquatic Animal Health in Aquaponics** — H. Yavuzcan Yildiz, V. Radosavljevic, G. Parisi, A. Cvetkovikj (p. 435). Biosecurity hazards and preventive approaches given restricted antimicrobial/antiparasitic use in aquaponics.
18. **Commercial Aquaponics: A Long Road Ahead** — M. Turnšek, R. Morgenstern, I. Schröter, M. Mergenthaler, S. Hüttel, M. Leyer (p. 453). Critically assesses economic-viability claims using literature plus current European commercial-farmer experience (horticulture, aquaculture, and market-response angles).
19. **Aquaponics: The Ugly Duckling in Organic Regulation** — P.R. Kledal, B. König, D. Matulić (p. 487). Examines the unresolved question of organic certification for aquaponics under EU/US regimes.
20. **Regulatory Frameworks for Aquaponics in the European Union** — T. Reinhardt, K. Hoevenaars, A. Joyce (p. 501). Uses Germany as a case study of construction/operation/commercialisation regulation and EU policy fit.
21. **Aquaponics in the Built Environment** — G. Proksch, A. Ianchenko, B. Kotzen (p. 523). Classifies commercial aquaponic farms by building-enclosure typology (greenhouse vs. indoor) for urban-integration and life-cycle-assessment purposes.

Part V — Aquaponics and Education
22. **Aquaponics as an Educational Tool** — R. Junge, T. Griessler Bulc, D. Anseeuw, H. Yavuzcan Yildiz, S. Milliken (p. 561). Strategies for embedding aquaponics in STEM curricula, illustrated with cross-country case studies.
23. **Food, Sustainability, and Science Literacy in One Package? ... a Danish Perspective** — B.E. Mikkelsen, C.M. Bosire (p. 597). Reports on the "Growing Blue & Green" (GBG/eGBG) programme in Danish municipal schools, drawing on **three original empirical/exploratory studies** (an exploratory study, a teacher feasibility study, and a digital-sensor-augmented eGBG study) — this chapter contains its own primary/exploratory data, unlike most others in the volume (see "Notable chapters for follow-up" below).
24. **Aquaponics and Social Enterprise** — S. Milliken, H. Stander (p. 607). Surveys social-enterprise aquaponics case studies worldwide (UK/Palestine "Byspokes" project, FAO Gaza Strip programme, INMED South Africa, Eden Aquaponics, Growing Power/Sweet Water Organics/CERES in the US/Australia) and synthesises Laidlaw & Magee's (2016) four survival factors for such enterprises. Read in full for this note.
